# Pro Medicus FY2016 — A Perfect Score, Five Years Early

*Supplementary page for: [Can You Spot a Great Company Before It Takes Off?](/)*

Pro Medicus (PME) is a medical imaging software company. It makes Visage — a platform that hospital radiology departments use to view and process medical scans. In 2016, it was a small company. By 2021, it had grown 10.96x.

The main experiment used FY2019–2022 data to build the rubric. PME was scored on its FY2016 annual report — five years earlier, using the exact same frozen five-signal rubric — as an out-of-sample test.

**Score: 100 out of 100.**

Every signal was present in FY2016. Every signal was present again in FY2017. This page shows exactly what DeepSeek Flash found in the FY2016 annual report.

---

## Signal 1 — Order Book

**Result: Present. Growing strongly.**

PME had a forward contracted revenue figure — not a vague pipeline, but legally binding contracts already signed for future years.

Verbatim from the FY2016 annual report:
> *"Forward contracted revenue doubled over the past 12 months and now exceeds $100M AUD over the next five years."*

At the time, PME's annual revenue was approximately $27.8M. The contracted forward revenue was **3.6 times** annual revenue — meaning the company had already locked in more work than it would earn in the next three and a half years.

The year before (FY2015), that forward revenue figure was approximately $50M. It doubled in twelve months.

---

## Signal 2 — Margin Expansion

**Result: Present. Expanding.**

Operating margin moved from 28.6% (FY2015) to 34.2% (FY2016) — a six-percentage-point improvement in a single year.

The company's own reporting:
> *"Operating Profit Before Interest and Income Tax $9,441k +87.9% (2015: $5,025k +121.1%)"*

Revenue grew 56.9% that year. Operating profit grew 87.9%. Profit growing faster than revenue is the definition of margin expansion — the business was scaling more profitably as it grew.

---

## Signal 3 — Execution Tone

**Result: Present. Execution-focused. Confidence higher than typical.**

The language in the FY2016 annual report was grounded in specific delivery milestones — sites deployed, contracts signed, implementation timelines. Not aspirational.

The model's assessment: *"execution-focused, higher confidence than typical for this report type, consistent with prior year."*

The language was also notably consistent with FY2015 — management wasn't shifting their story, revising their strategy, or softening their commitments.

---

## Signal 4 — Forward Guidance Specificity

**Result: Present. Quantified.**

Rather than vague forward statements ("we expect continued growth"), the FY2016 report contained specific, numbered forward commitments:

- Forward contracted revenue: $100M+ over the next five years (cited above)
- Revenue had "doubled over the past 12 months"
- Implementation pipeline with named sites and expected go-live timelines

The model classified forward guidance specificity as: *"quantified"* (the highest level).

---

## Signal 5 — Organic Revenue Growth

**Result: Present. 56.9% organic.**

Revenue grew from $17.7M (FY2015) to $27.8M (FY2016) — 56.9% growth, entirely organic. No acquisitions that year. Growth came from new hospital sites going live on the Visage platform and expansion of existing accounts.

The model's assessment: *"organic — growth driven by new Visage installations across North America and Europe, not acquisitions."*

## What Happened Next

PME's share price:
- FY2016: $5.08
- FY2021: $55.63
- **Return: 10.96x**

The rubric would have flagged PME as a top candidate in 2016. The five signals that defined the training set's winners — built from 2021–2026 data — were already fully present in 2016, in a different time period, in a different sector (software, not engineering).

That's what "out-of-sample" means in practice: the same patterns, earlier, elsewhere.
